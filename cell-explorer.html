How big are these things, really?

All three specimens drawn to true relative scale: an animal cell at 20 µm, a bacterium at 2 µm, a virus at 100 nm. Step through the three fields of view and watch what disappears. This is the single most-missed idea on cell projects: a bacterium is about a tenth the width of an animal cell, and a virus is about a twentieth the width of the bacterium.

A Texas-sized analogy

Scale the animal cell up to AT&T Stadium, roughly 275 meters across. At that scale the bacterium is about 27 meters, the length of two school buses parked nose to tail somewhere on the field. The virus? About 1.4 meters. A middle schooler standing next to those buses, easy to miss from the stands.
